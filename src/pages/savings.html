How savings work

Where the money comes from

Solar saves money in three ways. How much each one matters depends on your utility's rules.

☀️

1. You buy less power

Every kilowatt-hour your panels produce while you're using it is one you don't buy from the utility. At EIA's 2024 average of 16.5¢/kWh, that adds up quickly.

🔁

2. Credit for extra power

Many utilities offer net metering or export credits for the surplus you send to the grid at midday. Rules and credit values vary by state and utility.

🔋

3. Dodge peak prices

On time-of-use plans, a battery lets you use stored solar in the evening when grid power costs the most.

Financing

Three ways to go solar

Your advisor shows all three side by side with real numbers for your home.

Cash

Highest lifetime savings

  • You own the system from day one
  • Fastest payback, biggest long-term return
  • Any incentives you qualify for go to you
  • Largest upfront cost

Solar loan

Own it with $0 down

  • $0-down options available
  • You own the system and its value
  • Payment can be close to your current bill
  • Interest reduces total savings vs. cash

Lease / PPA

Lowest commitment

  • Little or nothing upfront
  • Maintenance typically included
  • You don't own the system
  • Smaller savings; contract transfers on sale

Availability, rates and terms depend on credit approval and location. Incentive programs change often — your advisor checks what applies at your address.
